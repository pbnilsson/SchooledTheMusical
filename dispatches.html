Update #4 · August 2026

Casting opens for Pittsburgh — and what playwriting teaches a musical.

Schooled Community!

This is the fourth update to the Schooled community as its journey continues. Here we go!

Casting!

I have an ask! We are casting for Pittsburgh! Know any performers? Casting details are here (12 roles, diverse cast, performer age skews young: 18–30s), and I’m presently circulating the casting call through Pittsburgh-area contacts. If you know any actors in Pittsburgh who might fit a part, please pass this along to them. Also, if you know people who are connected to the Pittsburgh musical theater scene and may be able to circulate the posting in their networks, I would be grateful if you would share this with them. Thank you!

Pittsburgh event details

I’m happy to share that the event hosting the staged reading of Acts I and II of Schooled is called Elevate Learning, a full-day professional learning experience for Pennsylvania teachers. Schooled is the pre-conference performance, and it is a standalone event the evening beforehand. Elevate Learning is hosted by the Pennsylvania Association of Independent Schools and Carlow University — and supported by The Grable Foundation, with some further support also being confirmed that I’ll be excited to share in the next update. The presentation of Schooled will be 6pm on Sunday evening, October 18, at the Rosemary Heyl Theatre in Carlow University’s Antonian Hall, and it is available to attend as a separate event from the conference the next day. I’ll send more information in the next update for those who would like to attend.

Playwriting vs. musical writing

By this past spring, I had spent about two and a half years diving deeply into the literature of musical theater: craft books, librettos, recordings, scores, and more. And by this time I had begun thinking with deeper intention about how story and character come together, especially in Act Two — so I started turning toward the literature of playwriting, which has a narrower focus than musical writing. In conversation with a playwright, though, I suddenly felt like a novice all over again, like I didn’t even have the right questions yet to think about plays as a distinct form from musicals. The playwright kindly directed me to the plays and essays of Marsha Norman, who is both a Pulitzer Prize–winning playwright and a Tony Award–winning book writer for musicals. I was quickly immersed in Norman’s works. In playwriting, I came to learn, the dialogue is the meat of the thing. In musicals, the dialogue is the appetizer to the music. It doesn’t mean dialogue is any less craft-full, but it does mean it serves a different purpose. In plays, the moments of great emotional tension and release happen in dialogue. In musicals, these moments happen in song.

What strikes me as particularly interesting, though, is the emergence of a tradition that probably began back with Gilbert and Sullivan, took on clear language in Meredith Willson’s description of his writing as “rhythmic dialogue” in The Music Man, and has probably reached its current peak form in Lin-Manuel Miranda’s work: something akin to potent scenes taking place in rhythmically scripted dialogue over a musical accompaniment. These aren’t moments of sung expression of unbridled emotion that happen in traditional songs, and may read more akin to scenes delivered in a composed spoken word/rap/recitative style, but they carry emotional potency nonetheless. A few of the numbers I’m working on in Act Two have this feel, and it has been a delight to look more closely at the pacing of climaxes in plays for lessons.

Branching into plays has also felt essential given the characters in Schooled. Writing for a strong female lead and diverse characters feels like it carries the responsibility to understand feminist and multicultural traditions that have existed in the theater, especially writing as a man who does not carry traditional markers of diversity. Marsha Norman’s writing opened the door to a lineage of writers of feminist theater that I hadn’t read before, reaching from Bess Wohl’s recent play Liberation, which won this year’s Pulitzer, back to Wendy Wasserstein, Paula Vogel, and others. I’m still discovering more — and I welcome recommendations. Similarly, August Wilson has been a huge gap in my reading, and his works and biography are on my nightstand. Going even further back, discovering the works of James Weldon Johnson has been revelatory — and deeply inspiring. More on Johnson in a future update! All this is cataloged in the reading list.

Seven weeks to go!

Yikes! Progress continues apace: Act Two is coming together, with some thorny scenes and songs taking their own sweet time, but I’m sooo happy with the way some of the songs for Act Two — which is set ten years after Act One — have taken the emotional valence to entirely new levels. Lots of work still to go — and even still, I’m reminding myself that what we’re preparing for is a staged reading. The show will inevitably undergo huge changes in the time after this presentation, but the presentation will be the fullest accumulation yet of story, song, and character development: the first presentation of the two acts together. It’s a form full enough to give a sense of the shape of the thing, but still raw enough to be molded in the time ahead by the care of a team. I am so excited.

Thank you for following along — and for sharing this with others who may be interested!

Peter

Update #3 · July 2026

Perspiration, collaboration, and continued education.

Schooled Community!

This is the third update to the Schooled community. Here we go!

Writing and revising: the creative process

Buckets of research suggest that the creative process has five stages: incubation, experimentation, perspiration, refinement, and integration. Ideas start by incubating: before we know what we’re thinking, ideas percolate deep in our minds, inspired by the many sources of information we take in. Once they begin to hatch, we experiment with those ideas. In teaching and learning, for example, we ask what different approaches we might take to teach a topic or text. In painting, we sketch out studies of different portions of an image. In music, we try out different melodies, harmonies, rhythms, or song structures. Eventually, we land on a form, and it takes perspiration to execute it: to finish the draft, to write the novel we’ve outlined, to complete the research we’ve designed. Then, once we have a draft, we refine it. And once the final touches have been made, we integrate it into our lives: we bring it into discourse with others and the world. The path isn’t always linear — it is full of backtracking and false starts and roundabouts — but it moves generally in that direction.

Act II is deep in the perspiration stage. It has an outline, a script has been written, and songs are now taking shape. There’s still plenty of experimentation (How might this song evolve? Maybe the melody should go in a different direction?), and I’m still devouring inspiration to keep new ideas incubating (see the reading list below) — but mostly, the current moment is about putting the pen on the page. And it is so fun and fulfilling. (And also hard, but happily so!) This is the period of turning an idea from an outline into a thing in the world. I can’t wait to share Act II with you!

The joys of collaboration

It has also been so exciting and so helpful to bring other minds together on this. Rian Alfiero has been an incredible friend and regular collaborator since day one of this project, offering insight, reflections, encouragement, and the kind of consistent support that I feel fortunate to have. We meet weekly like a writers’ group, sharing updates on our projects — and I absolutely wouldn’t have made progress on Schooled without this kind of accountability and support. (Rian has meanwhile been working on his own projects; here’s one…!)

I’ve also met twice now with director Ryan Dobrin and music director Mike Mackey. Ryan’s dramaturgical guidance has helped me think through storyline and character development in Act II. Our meetings remind me of show after show that I’ve read about which went through a process of asking: what is the central question, relationship, or tension that drives the show, and how can you focus every moment on advancing that question, relationship, or tension? Lin-Manuel Miranda cut a (killer) John Adams rap out of Hamilton because Adams wasn’t central to Hamilton and Burr’s conflict. Meredith Willson cut a longstanding character out of The Music Man because the character wasn’t central to the romance. Great shows are taut and economical with the little time they have to tell a rich story.

And conversations with Mike Mackey have surfaced new insights and angles into the music for Act II. I feel like a student again. Mike has deep history in music directing for musical theater and choirs — and also in jazz, which was his doctoral work. I studied jazz a little in college and faked it in NYC for a period. Now, with some pointers from Mike, I’m digging back into contemporary harmonic approaches and learning about the histories of songs I’ve been using as references for certain pieces. What an absolute joy. I’m beyond grateful to these collaborators — and others, too, who have pointed me toward new sources.

My reading list (on an updated website)

I’ve also updated the website for Schooled! Philosophically, I wanted it to model or reflect what happens in schools: I wanted to make my learning visible. So I’ve posted each of my updates there. And I’ve included the full curriculum I’ve taken up for myself since I began this work. You can visit the reading list page, which includes not only what I’m currently reading, but also what’s on the nightstand, and everything I’ve read, seen, watched, and listened to in order to study and develop this specific art form since I embarked on this work. Recommendations welcome, by the way! If you’re a musical theater aficionado (or an enthusiast, or even a curious consumer), I’d be glad to know what resonates for you or what you recommend.

Being a producer

July has also been the month of working with additional collaborators to bring this performance to fruition. I love making the art. But that art is more meaningful when it exists in the world. And making it actually happen in the world is the work of a producer, which is often a vaguely and inconsistently defined title. In this context, I’m describing a producer as someone who produces something, who makes it happen. This means arranging rehearsal space, identifying and coordinating with the performance venue, raising funds, managing budgets, planning the A/V and tech support, promotion, and more. This has been a layer underneath this whole process. I’ll share more about this work in August, including some of the brilliant, generous, and passionate advisors and partners who are helping bring this to life.

What’s next?

By the next update, we should have a casting call out, an announcement about the details of the event, and more to share, of course, about the writing. I look forward to the next!

Peter

Update #2 · June 2026

A team comes together, and Act II takes shape.

Schooled Community!

It’s been a busy month in Schooled land. Here we go!

Pittsburgh, October 18

We’re preparing for the staged reading coming up October 18, in Pittsburgh, PA. More details to come (probably in the next update) — but in the meantime, if you’re interested in attending, keep Sunday evening, October 18, in Pittsburgh on your calendar. It will be the first staged reading of both Act I and Act II, and I couldn’t be more grateful for all the support and interest around this.

Director!

I am beyond thrilled to share that the reading will be directed by Ryan Dobrin. Ryan has extraordinary experience both with early-stage work and with full Broadway productions. On Broadway, he was the associate director for the Tony Award–winning revival of Merrily We Roll Along, with Daniel Radcliffe, Jonathan Groff, and Lindsay Mendez, and he’s also a co-producer for Mexodus, which recently seemed to sweep through the off-Broadway award season. And what I’m most excited about is his dramaturgical work, close-reading shows. Schooled is a young show, and I’m very much looking forward to looking closely at what changes it needs in order to become its best version of itself.

Music Director!

One thing I learned in Austin is that directing the music of a show is a real skill in itself. (Well, I learned it back in NYC, too.) And while I have some music experience, I’m decidedly an amateur at coaching singers, giving feedback, and coordinating all the music components — all on a tight timeline. So I am so grateful and excited that Mike Mackey will be directing the music at the Pittsburgh reading. Mike has music-directed and arranged orchestrations for large shows like Les Misérables and Hello, Dolly!, and he’s directed choirs and choruses of all sizes. And what I’m most excited about is that he also has a PhD in Jazz Studies. Since jazz textures and harmonies play an important role in Act 2, I’m very much looking forward to working closely with him over the summer.

Writing!

And perhaps most importantly: the writing is taking shape! During May, I wrote out a book version of Act II — taking the outline I’ve had for almost a year and turning it (finally!) into dialogue. Then, since the beginning of June, I’ve been “songspotting,” finding the moments when music makes sense and building those moments into songs. Early scenes have focused on the prologue for Act 2 and a tense reunion of two characters partway into the act — a scene which, like a tango, mixes intimacy with distance. I’m excited for you all to hear it when the time comes! June, July, and August will be a sprint of songwriting. There’s a lot to get through, and I’m keeping as much of my calendar clear as possible to finish it all.

What’s teaching it

For inspiration, I’ve been reading: the developmental history of Cabaret (the parallels are striking!) and Aladdin (a reminder that big shows sometimes don’t work in draft stage), The Playwright’s Guidebook (full of useful nuggets), “40 Questions for a Political Play” (an excellent essay by Dan O’Brien), Second Act Trouble (a collection of cautionary tales), and more. For every text I read, I catalog the lessons learned — gathered on the reading list.

I’m about to hop on a plane and finish that book on Cabaret — and then get home and back to writing. More to come, and thank you for following along!

Peter

Update #1 · May 2026

An enormous success at SXSW EDU — and a full reading booked for Pittsburgh.

Schooled Community!

This is the first update as the journey takes its next step. Here we go!

Schooled at SXSW EDU

I’m so happy to share that the staged reading at SXSW EDU this past March was an enormous success. The actors and team in Austin put together an amazing staged reading in just a week; it was a full house when it started, and it just got fuller as the show went on. Then people were talking about it for days. And for those who like data: the post-show survey had an NPS of 67 (!), with near-full participation from the audience.

Next stop: Pittsburgh!

I’m thrilled to share that Schooled has now been scheduled for a full staged reading of both Acts I & II for Sunday evening, October 18, in Pittsburgh! Two generous funders have committed to supporting the event, and we have a large theater booked — so mark your calendars now. I’ll be sending more information in the weeks and months ahead.

Building a team

With five and a half months until the performance, my first deadline is to lock in the local team. Fall calendars are already filling for in-demand artists, so I just spent four days in New York City meeting directors for this reading, with calls scheduled with music directors in Pittsburgh. Once these key players are in place, I’ll be digging back into the story outline to finish the script and music.

Wait — the script and score aren’t finished?!

They’re fully outlined — but nope, not finished! A few reasons why: it’s really helpful to have a deadline; once a director is on board, I’ll have a crucial creative collaborator to help inform Act II (just as Deonté Warren was an incredible collaborator in Austin); and I learned a lot from the SXSW EDU reading — it made sense not to build out too much before knowing whether Act II would be a worthwhile endeavor. Turns out Act II is a worthwhile endeavor!

More broadly: did you know that Hamilton was booked at the Public Theater with most of Act II incomplete? Or that Sondheim’s Pulitzer Prize–winning Sunday in the Park with George was still missing two songs just days before opening night? Musicals are enormously complex, and one element of successful shows is the deep engagement and commitment of a full creative and producing team. My solo work won’t be complete — a single artist’s never is in musical theater.

So: I’m building the team now for this fall, and returning to writing. I look forward to keeping you updated — and let me know if you have questions!

Pete
